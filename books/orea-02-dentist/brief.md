# Brief — Orea and the dentist

Series:       Book 2. The reader already knows Orea and Whoof-Whoof from book 1.
              Do not introduce them.

Child:        Orea, four, a girl. Asks a great many questions about everything.
Companion:    Whoof-Whoof, a beige toy dog with long floppy ears — constant,
              appears on every page. Orea brings him along to show him things.
Family:       Mum. (Dad exists but is not in this story.)
Dentist:      A woman, warm and a bit playful. She has a favourite part of her
              own job. She explains things to children rather than over them.

Settings:     park    — the long way home, where the conversation happens
              surgery — a friendly dentist's room the next day: pictures all
                        over the walls, a big blue chair that goes up and down,
                        a tray of tools, a little set of pretend teeth

The problem:  Orea is frightened of the check-up that is coming. She saw the
              tools once and did not like them.

The method:   She meets every tool before it is used — each one is named and
              shown on the pretend teeth first. Afterwards she holds the little
              mirror and counts her own teeth out loud.

Pages:        about 10, more if a beat needs its own page
Reading age:  3-4
Tone:         warm, told aloud, plenty of dialogue, present tense

Must include: Mum's reassurance is CONCRETE — what will happen, step by step,
              and that Orea may hold her hand the whole time. Not "there is
              nothing to worry about".
              A page of real fun: the chair going up and down.
              Whoof-Whoof gets a check-up too, before Orea does.
              Something to take home at the end.

Never:        never lose or harm Whoof-Whoof; never promise it will not hurt;
              never say there is nothing to be scared of; Mum may be held onto
              the whole time, but Orea climbs into the chair herself; no
              needles, no drilling, no pain.
